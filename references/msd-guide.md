# MSD Guide

This reference gives the operational frame for the Metodo Strutturale Dinamico (MSD), based on Jorge Pompei e collaboratori, *Teoria e pratica del Metodo Strutturale Dinamico*, CEHBA, 2008/06, and the Spanish original *Teoria y practica del Metodo Estructural Dinamico*.

For bibliographic references, source hierarchy, and citation guidance, read `sources.md`.

## Core Sequence

The practical method can be reduced for agent use to three large moments:

1. **La Domanda**: delimit the problem, formulate the question, define Object-Interest.
2. **L'Analisi**: locate the object statically and study it dynamically through process, relations, and composition.
3. **La Risposta**: describe, summarize, synthesize, and answer the original question.

## Key Concepts

**Problem**: the initially vague or complex situation that needs clarification. It must be delimited before answering.

**Question**: the precise formulation of what the study must clarify.

**Object of Study**: what is being studied. It must be specific enough to locate in fields, process, relations, and composition.

**Interest**: the viewpoint or sense from which the object is observed. The same object changes when the interest changes.

**Static Analysis**: spatial or structural location of the object in major, middle, and minor fields.

**Dynamic Analysis**: temporal and relational study of the object through process, relations, and composition.

**Synthesis**: not a summary. It is a new formulation of the object obtained by relating differences and weighting them by the fixed interest.

**Response**: the conclusion that answers the initial question and clarifies the delimited problem.

## Practical Guardrails

- Keep the interest fixed. If it changes, the study changes.
- Do not confuse the object with the desired outcome.
- Do not reduce dynamic analysis to a list. Each lens must show movement or relation.
- Do not call a recap a synthesis. A synthesis must produce a new view.
- A useful response should orient action, but the action must derive from the synthesis.

## Compact Gate Checklist

1. Context is sufficient or assumptions are explicit.
2. Problem is delimited.
3. One central question is formulated.
4. Object-Interest is fixed.
5. Major, middle, and minor fields are identified.
6. Process, relations, and composition are analyzed.
7. Synthesis gives a new view.
8. Response answers the question and clarifies the problem.
